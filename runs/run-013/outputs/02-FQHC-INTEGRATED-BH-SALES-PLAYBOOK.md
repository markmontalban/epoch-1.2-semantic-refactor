# FQHCs with Integrated Behavioral Health — Tier 1 Sales Playbook

Last updated: 2026-07-20 15:40:22 PDT — edited by: Codex

This is the independent comparison route: it tests whether the staff-wellbeing
thesis survives a primary-care governance and workflow context.

## Decision snapshot

- **Tier and rationale:** Tier 1; FQHC reporting and integrated behavioral-health workflows are sufficiently distinct from CCBHCs to test whether one sales motion would be misleading. **Public fact + inference.**
- **Working confidence:** Medium for organizational legibility; low for sponsor, funded problem, staff fit, and the degree of difference from CCBHC buying paths. **Unknown.**
- **Advance only if:** Public qualification finds a care-team or workforce initiative with a staff-only use moment and a plausible operations/integration/workforce decision path. **Agent hypothesis.**
- **Park if:** The organization needs patient engagement, billable care, EHR integration, screening support, or evidence that a staff practice changes UDS or reimbursement results. **Claim boundary.**

## Sales motion

- **Position the offer:** “A voluntary, brief staff-wellbeing practice that respects the pace of primary care and stays outside the visit, handoff, and record.” **Agent hypothesis.**
- **Keep the ICP narrow:** Focus on FQHCs that publicly describe integrated behavioral health plus workforce, learning, or care-team support—not every health center offering behavioral-health services. **Agent hypothesis.**
- **Watch for triggers:** Integration expansion, care-team learning, behavioral-health staffing context, and public workforce initiatives are starting points for qualification, not evidence of intent. **Public context + inference.**
- **Map functions, not people:** Operations/workforce may own feasibility, integration/medical leadership the workflow boundary, quality/IT/privacy the implementation veto, and finance/grants/procurement the approval route. **Agent hypothesis.**
- **Use discovery, not persuasion:** Ask where teams lose access to existing support, which function owns the staff experience, whether a short practice is additive, and what patient-flow or privacy constraint rules it out; do not claim it improves UDS, access, clinical outcomes, or reimbursement. **Internal guidance.**

## Pilot design

- **Cohort boundary:** A voluntary behavioral-health or integrated-care staff cohort is the starting concept; never enroll patients or make use part of a clinical workflow. **Claim boundary.**
- **Safe use moment:** Consider optional learning, break, before/after-shift, or protected non-visit time; exclude visits, warm handoffs, charting, screening, and billing. **Agent hypothesis.**
- **Implementation owner:** A confirmed workforce/operations owner plus integrated-care and medical review, followed by privacy/IT/quality and finance review, is required before any evaluation design. **Unknown.**
- **Feasibility measures:** Assess activation, accessibility, workday fit, implementation burden, and perceived additivity selected with the center; do not use patient, quality-reporting, or payment outcomes. **Agent hypothesis.**
- **Pass / fail / stop:** Pass if a live staff-support problem, safe cohort, role path, and bounded next decision are confirmed; fail if current supports are sufficient or no owner exists; stop on patient data, clinical claims, EHR dependence, or unauthorized external action. **Internal decision rule.**

## Marketing blueprint

- **Positioning statement:** “A staff-support practice built for integrated care teams that cannot add another patient-facing task.” This is a draft hypothesis, not tested positioning. **Agent hypothesis.**
- **Content angles:** Explore care-team flow versus staff support; explain how behavioral-health integration changes internal review; and share a no-patient-data pilot-readiness framework. **Source-backed hypotheses.**
- **LinkedIn / association route:** Hypothesize professional thought leadership and health-center/association education around integrated-team implementation boundaries; no external action is authorized. **Marketing hypothesis.**
- **Owned-channel route:** Hypothesize an FQHC landing page, care-team fit checklist, and short email learning sequence; none is approved for production or delivery. **Marketing hypothesis.**
- **CTA and learning:** Draft CTA: “Check whether an optional staff practice can fit without touching patient flow”; future learning is workflow relevance and decision-path clarity, never patient or financial outcomes. **Draft-only.**

## Evidence and agent brief

- **Reporting context:** [HRSA UDS staffing/utilization](https://bphc.hrsa.gov/data-reporting/uds-training-and-technical-assistance/staffing-utilization) — primary, reviewed 2025; supports integrated-service reporting context, not a purchase need.
- **Program context:** [HRSA health-center data](https://data.hrsa.gov/topics/health-centers) — primary, 2024 data; supports the community health-center model, not local workflow fit.
- **Support ecosystem:** [HRSA resource clearinghouse](https://bphc.hrsa.gov/technical-assistance/health-center-resource-clearinghouse) — primary, reviewed 2025; supports existing TA breadth, not a gap.
- **Workflow example:** [HRSA integration story](https://bphc.hrsa.gov/about-health-centers/health-center-stories/wisconsin-behavioral-health) — primary example; supports a possible integrated-care configuration, not general buyer behavior.
- **Internal comparison:** [RUN-012 scorecard](../../run-012/outputs/SEGMENT-PRIORITIZATION-SCORECARD.md) — internal, 2026-07; supports backup ranking only. See the [matrix](SOURCES.md).
