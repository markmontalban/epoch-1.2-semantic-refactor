# Family-Caregiver Support Organizations — Tier 2 Sales Playbook

Last updated: 2026-07-20 15:40:22 PDT — edited by: Codex

The buyer is a caregiver-support channel, while the end user is an unpaid
caregiver. The resource must add choice without shifting care-system burden
onto the caregiver.

## Decision snapshot

- **Tier and rationale:** Tier 2; established caregiver-support networks and resource channels exist, but the funding and adoption route for an optional practice is unknown. **Public fact + unknown.**
- **Working confidence:** Medium for channel plausibility; low for digital-resource ownership, caregiver fit, and non-harmful positioning. **Unknown.**
- **Advance / park:** Advance only where a specific program has a voluntary resource channel and appropriate caregiver advisory context; park when the need is respite, navigation, financial help, crisis response, or clinical care. **Claim boundary.**

## Sales motion

- **Position the offer:** An opt-in self-directed resource within a caregiver-support menu, never a substitute for respite, navigation, benefits, care coordination, or professional help. **Agent hypothesis.**
- **Qualify the trigger:** Published resource libraries, group support, navigator programs, or digital-access work may justify research; caregiver burden statistics do not establish fit. **Public fact + inference.**
- **Map and discover:** Hypothesize program/caregiver-support leader, caregiver advisory voice, and executive/funder as relevant functions; ask which caregiver group is served, what resource gap exists, and whether choice is possible without disclosure. **Agent hypothesis.**
- **Objection / guardrail:** “This asks caregivers to solve a structural support gap themselves” is valid disconfirmation; never imply health improvement, reduced burden, or replacement of services. **Claim boundary.**

## Pilot design

- **Bounded concept:** One opt-in resource channel, no caregiver profile/data requirement, clear referral/safety boundary, and user-selected access. **Agent hypothesis.**
- **Feasibility test:** Confirm language, accessibility, cultural fit, resource navigation, advisory review, and a channel owner before any pilot-design discussion. **Unknown.**
- **Stop condition:** Stop if the program needs clinical/crisis functionality, personal data, or a claim that the practice resolves caregiving burden. **Internal decision rule.**

## Marketing blueprint

- **Position and angles:** Draft position: “one optional moment of support in a wider caregiver-resource system”; explore choice without blame, digital-resource fit, and the limits of technology. **Draft hypotheses.**
- **Channel hypotheses:** Caregiver associations, owned resource libraries/email, and local navigator/program channels are possible future routes; no content, distribution, or monitoring is authorized. **Marketing hypotheses.**
- **Draft CTA / learning:** “Check whether this is a respectful optional resource”; future learning is channel fit, accessibility, and appropriateness—not burden or health outcomes. **Draft-only.**

## Evidence and agent brief

- **Network context:** [ACL caregiver collaborative](https://acl.gov/ncsc) — primary, 2026-06; supports capacity/service focus, not adoption.
- **Funding context:** [ACL strategy funding](https://acl.gov/news-and-events/announcements/funding-opportunity-advancing-state-implementation-national-strategy) — primary, 2026-06; supports policy activity, not local resources.
- **Channel context:** [Family Caregiver Alliance](https://www.caregiver.org/) — sector, current; supports navigation/support channels, not product fit.
- **Implementation context:** [Best Programs for Caregiving](https://bpc.caregiver.org/) — sector, current; supports program comparison, not new-program evidence. See [matrix](SOURCES.md).
