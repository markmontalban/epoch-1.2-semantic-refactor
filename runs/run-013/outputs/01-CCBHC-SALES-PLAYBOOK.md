# CCBHCs — Tier 1 Sales Playbook

Last updated: 2026-07-20 15:40:22 PDT — edited by: Codex

This is the deepest current route because CCBHCs have a legible operating
model—not because any CCBHC has shown interest in MindfulText.

## Decision snapshot

- **Tier and rationale:** Tier 1; SAMHSA formalizes staffing, access, care coordination, quality/reporting, and governance, making the operating environment more legible than the other contexts. **Public fact.**
- **Working confidence:** High for category legibility; low for actual need, budget, buying path, product fit, and willingness to evaluate. **Agent hypothesis / unknown.**
- **Advance only if:** A public organization-level review finds an owned workforce or staff-learning priority, a staff-only safe-use moment, and a plausible cross-functional review path. **Agent hypothesis.**
- **Park if:** The route requires clinical functionality, crisis coverage, EHR/reporting integration, mandatory use, or a promised workforce/quality result. **Claim boundary.**

## Sales motion

- **Position the offer:** “A voluntary, brief workday wellbeing practice for time-constrained care teams, designed to sit beside—not inside—existing workforce support.” **Agent hypothesis.**
- **Keep the ICP narrow:** Start with a CCBHC that publicly describes workforce development, staff learning, quality improvement, or a related internal-support initiative; exclude organizations where care delivery or reporting integration is the implied job. **Agent hypothesis.**
- **Watch for triggers:** CCBHC expansion/sustainability work, public workforce initiatives, learning programs, and quality-improvement language are qualification cues, not buying signals. **Public context + inference.**
- **Map functions, not people:** Executive/workforce owns mission and priority; clinical/quality validates workflow and boundaries; finance, privacy, IT, and procurement test a vendor path. Every role remains hypothetical until organization-level evidence says otherwise. **Agent hypothesis.**
- **Use discovery, not persuasion:** Ask which staff support exists, where it fails to fit the workday, what would be additive versus redundant, who must agree, and what constraint would stop even a small evaluation; never say MindfulText solves staffing, retention, documentation, PPS, or care quality. **Internal guidance.**

## Pilot design

- **Cohort boundary:** A voluntary staff cohort—not patients, crisis teams while on coverage, or a clinically mandated group—would be the only safe evaluation concept. **Claim boundary.**
- **Safe use moment:** Consider between-shift, break, onboarding, or optional learning windows; never place use inside crisis response, treatment, required documentation, or care coordination. **Agent hypothesis.**
- **Implementation owner:** A confirmed workforce/learning owner plus clinical/quality and privacy review is required before a pilot-design conversation; no buyer has been confirmed. **Unknown.**
- **Feasibility measures:** Test invitation clarity, voluntary activation, accessibility, reported workflow fit, and implementation burden selected with the organization; do not predefine outcome improvement. **Agent hypothesis.**
- **Pass / fail / stop:** Pass only if the buyer names a live problem, safe use moment, reviewers, and next design decision; fail for redundancy or no owner; stop on protected data, clinical use, promised outcomes, or external action without approval. **Internal decision rule.**

## Marketing blueprint

- **Positioning statement:** “A small staff-support layer for care teams whose work leaves little room for a workshop or app session.” This is draft-only language, not an externally validated claim. **Agent hypothesis.**
- **Content angles:** Explain why staff wellbeing must complement structural support; show how low-disruption learning is assessed without patient data; and clarify the difference between a staff resource and clinical/reporting technology. **Source-backed hypotheses.**
- **LinkedIn / association route:** Hypothesize short founder posts and association-session proposals about implementation boundaries and workforce-support design; no post, pitch, sponsorship, or monitoring is authorized. **Marketing hypothesis.**
- **Owned-channel route:** Hypothesize a CCBHC-specific website explainer, email sequence, and pilot-readiness checklist; do not build or publish them under this run. **Marketing hypothesis.**
- **CTA and learning:** Draft CTA: “Assess whether a voluntary staff-support layer fits your existing workforce work”; future learning would be relevance, safe-use fit, and role-path clarity—not leads, clicks, or outcomes. **Draft-only.**

## Evidence and agent brief

- **Criteria context:** [SAMHSA CCBHC criteria](https://www.samhsa.gov/communities/certified-community-behavioral-health-clinics/ccbhc-certification-criteria) — primary, updated 2026-01; supports program areas, not a purchase priority.
- **Care-model context:** [SAMHSA CCBHC overview](https://www.samhsa.gov/communities/certified-community-behavioral-health-clinics) — primary; supports 24/7/crisis and coordination constraints, not product fit.
- **Implementation context:** [CCBHC S-TAC](https://www.samhsa.gov/technical-assistance/ccbhc-s-tac) — primary; supports an implementation ecosystem, not vendor demand.
- **Structural guardrail:** [SAMHSA burnout guide](https://library.samhsa.gov/sites/default/files/pep22-06-02-005.pdf) — primary, 2022; supports organization-level strategy, not an individual-resource outcome claim.
- **Internal ranking:** [RUN-012 map](../../run-012/outputs/TOP-TEN-SEGMENT-MAP.md) — internal, 2026-07; supports prioritization only. The full source limitations are in the [matrix](SOURCES.md).
