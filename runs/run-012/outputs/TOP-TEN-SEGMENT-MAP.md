# [[runs/run-012/RUN-012-Segment-Prioritization|RUN-012]] — Top-ten Segment Map

Last updated: 2026-07-20 10:45:00 PDT — edited by: Codex

This is a market-context map. It identifies distinct buyer/workflow contexts
for prioritization; it does not turn people or organizations into leads.

| Rank | Segment | Plausible organizational sponsor | Why it is distinct | Evidence status |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Certified Community Behavioral Health Clinics (CCBHCs) | Executive, workforce/HR, clinical operations, quality | A defined community-behavioral-health model with staffing, access, coordination, and reporting context | Strongest retained account evidence; current public context refreshed |
| 2 | FQHCs with integrated behavioral health | Executive, workforce/HR, integrated-care leadership | Primary-care governance and integrated behavioral-health workflow differ from CCBHCs | Existing comparison branch; current HRSA reporting context refreshed |
| 3 | Non-CCBHC community behavioral-health providers | Executive, workforce/HR, program leadership | Community-provider workforce context without the CCBHC certification structure | Plausible but less legible than #1 |
| 4 | HCBS/IDD providers employing DSPs | Workforce/HR, operations, residential/program leadership | Distinct frontline workforce and provider-sponsored route | Role/context supported; setting and sponsor unknown |
| 5 | Peer-support and recovery community organizations | Program, peer-workforce, executive leadership | Voluntary, person-centered peer-work boundaries are distinct from clinical care | Category context only; sponsor unclear |
| 6 | MHA affiliates / comparable support-program workforce | Affiliate/program leadership | Existing founder-evidence context, with specific non-clinical boundaries | Context anchor; no new-target authorization |
| 7 | Family-caregiver support organizations | Program, caregiver-support, community-partnership leadership | Individual caregiver need can be mediated through a support organization | Strong public context; adoption/funding route unknown |
| 8 | Nursing homes / skilled-nursing facilities | Administrator, HR, nursing leadership | Regulated long-term-care staffing environment | Strong staffing evidence; product-fit and claim risk are unfavorable |
| 9 | Home health and hospice providers | Operations, HR, clinical leadership | Mobile, distributed care work and LTSS staffing context | Broad workforce context; little MindfulText-specific evidence |
| 10 | Independent LCSWs / small therapy practices | Practice owner or individual practitioner | Individual/professional purchase route, not organization-sponsored workforce support | Historical hypothesis only; diffuse buyer and weak organization fit |

## Source boundaries

- The CCBHC and FQHC branches reuse the retained [[growth/SEGMENTS#seg-001--care-delivery-organizations|SEG-001]] / [[growth/SEGMENTS#seg-002--community-behavioral-health-providers|SEG-002]] /
  [[growth/SEGMENTS#seg-003--fqhcs-with-integrated-behavioral-health|SEG-003]], [[growth/CDP-001|CDP-001]], and [[runs/run-005/RUN-005-Organization-Registry|RUN-005]]–[[runs/run-008/RUN-008-Narrative-Pain-Map|RUN-008]] records.
- DSP employer context reuses [[runs/run-009/RUN-009-DSP-Qualification|RUN-009]]; federal context confirms a nationwide
  direct-care workforce shortage but does not establish product demand.
- Nursing-home and family-caregiver contexts are public-category signals only:
  CMS identifies staffing as a primary concern in nursing homes, and ACL
  documents a national caregiver-support strategy. Neither supports an offer,
  buyer, or outreach claim. [CMS](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/health-safety-standards/quality-safety-oversight-general-information/cms-nursing-home-staffing-campaign) · [ACL](https://acl.gov/CaregiverStrategy)
