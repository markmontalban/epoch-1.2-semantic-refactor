# RUN-006 — Segment adjacency summary

Last updated: 2026-07-15 00:55:00 PDT — edited by: Codex

## Map result

The center is `SEG-001 — Care-delivery organizations`. Within one direct
relationship, the research identifies 19 segment nodes, 6 representative
organizations already present in the RUN-005 registry, 17 adjacent ecosystem
nodes, and 8 system-level nodes: 50 nodes total.

`Under` means the node is a care-delivery subsegment or representative provider.
`Beside` means the node connects providers through professional, payment,
quality, technology, workforce, or convening infrastructure. `Above` means a
direct parent, regulator, funder, payer, or system-level operating environment.

## Strongest research branches

- **Community behavioral health / CCBHCs:** Closest fit to the existing GH-004 work; the category has explicit workforce, care-coordination, service, governance, and funding structures.
- **FQHCs / integrated behavioral health:** The most important comparison branch; it shares behavioral-health and social-work workflows but may have different governance and purchasing logic.
- **Distributed care:** Home health, hospice, and long-term/post-acute care are distinct workforce environments where a low-disruption format could be structurally relevant, but no demand is established.
- **Ecosystem channels:** National Council, NACHC, NASW, ANA, and provider associations connect to large provider communities, but they are channels or context—not assumed buyers.
- **System levers:** SAMHSA, CMS, HRSA, Medicaid, quality bodies, and workforce programs shape provider constraints and category language without becoming immediate target accounts.

## Decision-useful implication

The map supports keeping community behavioral health / CCBHCs as the lead
branch, while treating FQHC integrated behavioral health as the first controlled
comparison. Home health, hospice, and aging services are credible adjacent
branches but should be tested only after the buyer/workflow question is made
specific. Professional associations are more plausible as learning or channel
routes than as direct buyers.

## Evidence limits

Public sources establish organizational relationships, category definitions,
and visible workforce or operating context. They do not establish buyer
authority, budget, willingness to pay, current unmet need, efficacy, or a right
to contact any organization. The six named provider organizations are retained
from RUN-005 as comparison examples, not new purchase leads.
