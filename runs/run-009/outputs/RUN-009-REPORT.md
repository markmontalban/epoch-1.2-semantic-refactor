# RUN-009 Report — Direct Support Professional ICP qualification

Last updated: 2026-07-15 08:30:00 PDT — edited by: Codex

## Result

**Pass, with a bounded recommendation:** DSPs are distinct enough from LCSWs to warrant a candidate ICP, but not yet a separate market segment. The best current framing is a frontline user audience within `SEG-001 — Care-delivery organizations`, with the provider organization as the potential sponsor/buyer.

## What the evidence establishes

1. **DSP is a real occupational category, not generic caregiver shorthand.** The U.S. Department of Labor describes DSPs as skilled workers supporting people with disabilities in community participation, employment, activities of daily living, health and safety, advocacy, and emotional support. DSPs are part of the broader direct-care workforce. [DOL DSP overview](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/odep/program-areas/individuals/DSP)
2. **The role is broader and more continuous than the existing LCSW frame.** Public training material places DSP work across IDD and mental-health settings, residential and home-care providers, day habilitation, and frontline supervision. The emphasis is person-centered support, trauma-informed practice, emotional regulation, wellness, and stress reduction—not diagnosis or psychotherapy. [UNH START DSP course](https://iod.unh.edu/national-center-start-services/training-professional-development/idd-mh-professional-development-series/direct-support-professionals-course)
3. **Workforce strain is a credible research signal.** DOL identifies a nationwide shortage of direct-care workers including DSPs. NADSP describes turnover, low morale, recruitment, retention, workplace culture, and career ladders as central workforce issues; North Carolina has funded recruitment, retention, training, mentorship, and career-pathway responses. These are workforce-system signals, not proof that DSP employers want MindfulText. [NADSP workforce presentation](https://nadsp.org/services/training/keynote-presentations/), [North Carolina DSP initiative](https://www.ncdhhs.gov/divisions/mental-health-developmental-disabilities-and-substance-use-services/strengthening-north-carolinas-mental-health-substance-use-idd-and-tbi-workforce/direct-support-professional-initiative)
4. **MHA is a useful language and context anchor, not a DSP demand validation.** Public MHA material connects disability, mental health, peer/support roles, access, and wellness advocacy; the founder-supplied MHA Dutchess evidence adds a relevant organization-sponsored, low-disruption support precedent. Neither establishes transferability to DSP employers or DSP engagement. [MHA disability and mental health webinar](https://mhanational.org/calendar/serving-an-underserved-population-peer-support-disability-and-mental-health), [MHA workplace wellbeing context](https://www.flexjobs.com/employer-blog/webinar-create-culture-support-well-being-insights-mental-health-america)

## Comparison with prior Epoch 1 work

| Dimension | LCSW hypothesis | DSP working hypothesis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary role | Licensed clinical practitioner | Frontline, person-centered support worker |
| Work setting | Client-facing clinical care | Home, residential, community, employment, IDD, and behavioral-health support |
| Likely user pain | Clinical emotional load and occupational stress | Emotional load, safety/complexity, shift or coverage pressure, low autonomy, retention strain |
| Likely sponsor | Clinical, workforce, HR, or executive leader | Workforce/HR, operations, program, residential, or executive leader |
| Product boundary | Non-clinical stress-regulation support | Non-clinical, low-disruption reset/support; never a substitute for staffing, pay, supervision, crisis response, or clinical care |
| Evidence status | Candidate ICP-018 with prior research | New candidate audience; no direct MindfulText evidence yet |

## Recommended registry treatment

Add a future `ICP-022` candidate after Mark review, scoped to DSPs in IDD, behavioral-health, or home/community-based support settings. Keep the provider buyer separate from the worker audience. Do not add `SEG-004` yet: the evidence supports a distinct role, but not a distinct buyer/workflow boundary from `SEG-001`.

## Highest-leverage unknowns

- Which DSP setting has the clearest combination of emotional load, retention pressure, and an identifiable sponsor?
- Do provider leaders frame the problem as wellbeing, retention, safety, workforce capacity, quality, or something else?
- Can a brief text practice fit shift-based, mobile, residential, or community work without creating privacy, supervision, or clinical-role confusion?
- Would DSPs engage voluntarily, and would an organization sponsor the benefit?

## Evaluation

- **Pass:** Role definition, distinction from LCSWs, source-backed workforce signals, buyer/user separation, and next test are present.
- **Fail condition avoided:** The report does not treat DSPs as all care workers or convert workforce problems into product demand.
- **Inconclusive items:** Willingness to pay, adoption, efficacy, engagement, transferability from MHA, and the best DSP setting remain unproven.
