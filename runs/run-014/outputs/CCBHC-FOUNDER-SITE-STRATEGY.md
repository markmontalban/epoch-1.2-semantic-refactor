# CCBHC Founder Positioning & Asset Readiness

Last updated: 2026-07-20 21:04:00 PDT — edited by: Codex

## Recommendation

Build proof and a decision path before a campaign. MindfulText already has a
credible healthcare-professional presence, but its public site is primarily an
individual-subscription and partner-network experience. A CCBHC visitor needs
an organization-facing answer to four questions: what this is, where it fits,
where it does not fit, and why the organization should keep looking.

Start with a focused CCBHC landing page and one externally cleared MHA Dutchess
proof module. Follow with a founder point-of-view article and a short readiness
checklist. Do not begin with a paid campaign or broad blog cadence: neither
solves the current proof, category, and buyer-review gaps.

### Positioning to own

**A voluntary, text-based micro-practice for care teams that need a brief
moment of support without adding another app, workshop, patient task, or
reporting system.**

This is a positioning hypothesis, not an efficacy or purchase claim. The
explicit boundary is its strength: MindfulText sits beside workforce, learning,
and wellbeing efforts. It is not therapy, crisis support, clinical care,
patient engagement, documentation, EHR functionality, or a substitute for
staffing, compensation, supervision, or organizational change.

### Founder voice

> Care teams do not need another demand on their attention. They deserve
> structural support first—and, where it fits, an optional, low-friction way to
> pause and reset during the workday. MindfulText is designed for that smaller
> role.

Avoid "solve burnout," "improve retention," "reduce turnover," "transform
care," and "CCBHC-ready" unless a specific, supportable standard or customer
result justifies the wording.

## Website plan

### Primary page: `/ccbhc-staff-support/`

**Audience:** executive, workforce/learning, HR, operations, and clinical or
quality reviewers at CCBHCs. It is not for patients, crisis teams, or individual
subscriber conversion.

**Primary CTA (draft only):** `Assess staff-support fit`

| Section | Job | Draft-only copy direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hero | Establish a narrow offer and boundary. | **A small, optional staff-support layer for care teams.** Brief mindfulness prompts by text—built to fit beside existing workforce support, not inside clinical care. |
| Why text | Explain delivery without outcome claims. | No app to add. A brief prompt can arrive in an optional break, before or after a shift, or in a learning window chosen by the organization. |
| Appropriate use | Let a workforce owner self-qualify. | Consider MindfulText when you have a voluntary staff group, a protected non-care moment, and an existing workforce, learning, or wellbeing effort it can complement. |
| Not the job | Make safety and scope visible. | Not for patients, crisis coverage, treatment, care coordination, documentation, reporting, or staffing remediation. |
| Proof | Turn MHA Dutchess experience into credible context. | **How one community organization introduced a daily text-based mindfulness resource.** Use the approved CEO quote, exact approved result(s), dates, and a one-organization disclosure. |
| Review path | Lower reviewer uncertainty. | Workforce or learning may own fit; clinical/quality can validate boundaries; privacy, IT, finance, and procurement can review the appropriate implementation path. This is a buying-process hypothesis, not a claim about every CCBHC. |
| CTA | Offer a next step proportionate to evidence. | **See whether a voluntary staff-support layer fits your existing work.** |

### Proof module: MHA Dutchess

Make the case study a modular page section plus a standalone proof page. Include
only the organization's identity and CEO attribution, workforce/program setup,
one to three exact approved results or observations, a one-organization
limitation, and one implementation lesson. Do not display a percentage unless
the denominator, period, and approval are present in the cleared material.

### Navigation and conversion

- Add **For behavioral-health organizations** under the organization or partner
  pathway; keep consumer pricing separate.
- Put the MHA Dutchess proof module on the CCBHC page before moving it to the
  homepage.
- Link the readiness checklist from the page and founder article.
- Use one organization-facing CTA consistently. Do not use a free trial as the
  primary CCBHC CTA; it frames the product as an individual app rather than an
  organization-reviewed staff resource.

## Asset sequence

| Priority | Asset | Purpose | Draft-only title / hook |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | CCBHC landing page | Conversion and category clarity. | **Optional staff support for care teams—without another clinical workflow.** |
| 2 | MHA Dutchess proof page | Credible social proof, with controlled claims. | **What one community organization learned from a daily mindfulness-text program.** |
| 3 | Founder article | Establish Mark's point of view. | **Staff wellbeing is not a staffing plan. It can still be part of responsible support.** |
| 4 | Readiness checklist | Let organizations self-disqualify or prepare. | **Five questions before adding a staff-support resource.** |
| 5 | Founder post series | Distribute the point of view after the page exists. | `What a staff resource should never be asked to do`; `Why no-app support can be worth testing`; `The difference between support and another task`. |

### Readiness checklist content

1. Is there a voluntary, staff-only cohort—not patients or people in crisis?
2. Is there a protected break, between-shift, onboarding, or optional-learning
   moment?
3. Does the use complement workforce or wellbeing work rather than substitute
   for staffing, supervision, compensation, or clinical care?
4. Is there a workforce, learning, HR, or operations owner who can assess fit?
5. Can clinical/quality and privacy/IT reviewers confirm that no patient data,
   reporting, or care workflow is involved?

Any `no` should direct the reader to pause rather than convert.

## Founder content approach

Start with **one substantial article**, then repurpose its reasoning into three
short posts. The article should acknowledge structural causes of workforce
stress; explain the limited role of an optional, accessible practice; state the
staff-only/no-patient-data/non-clinical boundary; use the exact MHA Dutchess
proof; and invite readers to use the readiness checklist. This is more credible
than generic mindfulness tips or a high-volume blog calendar.

The National Council's CCBHC workforce material treats wellbeing as an
organizational responsibility requiring leadership and staff input. Align with
that context rather than implying a text program solves it.

## What to delay

- **Paid campaign:** wait until the landing page, proof module, and outcome
  attribution are live and a separate account/role qualification step defines a
  public audience.
- **Broad SEO blog program:** wait until one article and the checklist establish
  stable language and a conversion path.
- **Certification language:** do not imply support for certification, reporting,
  or compliance.

## Pre-publication claim checklist

- Verify every MHA Dutchess quote, logo, statistic, attribution, date, and
  claim against the cleared source and approval record.
- State the one-organization limitation near proof material.
- Keep the non-clinical, non-crisis, no-patient-data boundary visible.
- Remove any wording that promises reduced stress, burnout, turnover, or care
  outcomes unless the exact claim is authorized and supportable.
- Confirm CTA, form, data handling, and follow-up separately; this run does not
  authorize publication, collection, or outreach.

## Decision after this run

Advance to a separately approved build/publish decision only if the cleared MHA
Dutchess material supports an accurate proof module and Mark accepts the
staff-only CCBHC position. Otherwise, revise the position around the exact
case-study evidence before building a landing page or starting a campaign.
