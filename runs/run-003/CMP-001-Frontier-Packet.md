## 1. Testable hypothesis and disconfirmation

**Hypothesis:** When a community behavioral-health organization has a visible
workforce wellbeing or retention priority and staff have little time away from
care, a workforce, clinical, operations, or quality leader will consider a
small, asynchronous daily MindfulText support benefit relevant enough to have a
scoped pilot-design conversation.

**Principal disconfirmation:** The organization sees individual wellbeing
content as irrelevant, redundant, or counterproductive unless structural
problems—staffing, pay, workload, supervision, or culture—are addressed first,
and no responsible buyer sees a limited support benefit as worth evaluating.

## 3. Claim boundaries and proof needed

- MindfulText is a nonclinical support benefit, not therapy, diagnosis,
  treatment, crisis support, or a substitute for structural workforce change.
- Do not claim it reduces turnover, burnout, stress, or care-quality risk for
  a new organization. The MHA material is internal prioritization evidence,
  not transferable proof or an externally reusable testimonial until rights are
  confirmed.
- Before a pilot is proposed, establish the staff workflow, current supports,
  buyer owner, desired workforce outcome, success measure, and whether a
  low-disruption daily practice would be additive rather than redundant.

## 4. Discovery questions

1. Where in the workday do staff have the least practical time to use existing
   wellbeing or professional-development support?
2. Which workforce concern is most urgent now: retention, staff stress,
   administrative burden, morale, recruitment, or another issue?
3. What supports already exist, and where do they fail to fit staff workflow?
4. Which functions would need to agree before a staff-support benefit could be
   evaluated?
5. What would make a short, asynchronous text-based practice helpful,
   redundant, or inappropriate?
6. Which outcome would matter enough to measure in a small trial, and what
   would count as no meaningful improvement?
7. What implementation, privacy, clinical-boundary, or procurement constraint
   would prevent even a limited evaluation?

## 5. Signal, pass, fail, and stop

**Target signal:** Level 2 — a qualified organizational buyer confirms the
workflow problem, identifies the relevant buying-group path, and agrees to a
separate pilot-design or requirements conversation.

- **Pass:** A qualified leader names a live workforce problem, sees the
  low-disruption concept as plausibly additive, identifies the next decision
  participants, and accepts a bounded next conversation.
- **Fail:** The problem is not current, the concept duplicates a satisfactory
  support, no buyer owns it, or the organization rejects individual-support
  framing in favor of a different intervention.
- **Stop:** The conversation would require protected/private data, a clinical
  treatment claim, crisis use, outbound automation, or a promise the product
  cannot support.

## 6. Draft-only concept material

### A small workday support layer for care teams

Care-delivery teams often have little time for workshops or app sessions during
busy days. MindfulText is a proposed daily, asynchronous mindfulness practice
delivered by text and designed to be read in a brief workday window.

It is not a replacement for staffing, supervision, workload redesign, employee
assistance, or clinical care. The question for a prospective organization is
whether a lightweight support layer would be useful alongside its existing
workforce efforts, and what a responsible small evaluation would need to show.
