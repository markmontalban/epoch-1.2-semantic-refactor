## Recommended promise

> **See where a brief workday refocus may fit—and where it does not.**
>
> Answer six quick questions about a care team's workday.
>
> You'll receive practical guidance on possible uses for brief mindfulness by
> text, what respectful access requires, and when leadership action matters
> more.
>
> **No names. No employee or patient information.**

The reflection helps a leader consider where brief, optional mindfulness
prompts by text could be easy to introduce around a team's workday. MindfulText
may align foundational content to a selected workday moment and organization
language; it does not promise a bespoke program or organizational outcome.

It does not assess employee wellbeing, diagnose a person or team, grade an
organization, predict outcomes, or certify readiness. "Evidence-informed" is
not public copy in this version: its specific content basis and buyer meaning
need validation before that claim is used.

## Recommended questions

Use only fixed choices. Do not include free text in the first version. Each
answer should return useful guidance rather than a score, grade, or readiness
label.

1. **Which workday moment deserves more support?**
   - Before beginning a shift
   - Between demanding interactions
   - After an emotionally intense moment
   - During a protected break or learning window
   - At the transition out of work
   - There is no realistic moment right now
2. **What could a brief mindfulness practice usefully support in that moment?**
   - Pausing before reacting
   - Noticing stress or distraction
   - Returning attention to the present task
   - Transitioning between demanding parts of the day
   - Creating a more intentional beginning or ending
   - We are not sure yet

   Present these as possible uses, not promised outcomes.
3. **What would make the practice genuinely accessible to staff?** Choose all
   that matter:
   - No new app or account
   - A practice that takes only a few minutes
   - Private use without manager observation
   - A predictable or protected moment
   - Easy participation by text
   - Freedom to participate or decline
4. **Can staff use it with dignity and genuine choice?**
   - Yes — participation would be voluntary, private, and unrelated to performance
   - Partly — some expectations or privacy details need clarification
   - Not currently — participation could feel expected or monitored
5. **How would this sit beside the organization's responsibilities?**
   - As one staff resource alongside work on workload, safety, supervision, pay, and working conditions
   - The relationship is not yet clear
   - It may be expected to compensate for structural problems
6. **What would make a small pilot worth learning from?**
   - Whether staff find the format usable
   - Which workday moment fits best
   - Whether the prompts feel relevant and respectful
   - Whether the boundaries remain clear
   - Whether staff want continued access
   - We need help choosing one learning question

### Boundary confirmation

MindfulText would remain staff-only and nonclinical—not patient support, crisis
care, therapy, employee evaluation, or workforce surveillance.

## Recommended result

Do not calculate a score, percentage, grade, or readiness label. Build the
result directly from selected answers under six plain headings:

- **Your most promising moment** — reflect the selected workday moment; use
  "brief workday refocus" for the leader-facing guidance. An eventual
  staff-facing practice may instead use "brief workday pause" or "self-care
  moment."
- **What mindfulness may offer there** — describe the selected possible use
  carefully, without promising stress, retention, staffing, care, or other
  organization outcomes.
- **What would make access respectful** — summarize the selected conditions
  for a few-minute, private, voluntary, text-accessible experience.
- **What leadership still owns** — identify workload, staffing, safety,
  supervision, pay, and working conditions as responsibilities mindfulness
  must not be asked to solve.
- **One useful pilot question** — recommend one thing to learn, not a score.
  The default is: **Does this format feel private, voluntary, and easy to use
  in the selected workday moment?**
- **A two-minute example** — optionally offer a brief MindfulText-style
  practice before any identity request.

End every result with the same low-pressure choices, without optimizing a
conversion hierarchy:

- keep or save the guidance through a later approved anonymous-safe method;
- experience a personal MindfulText demo;
- request a short fit conversation; or
- leave without identifying.

The result must remain useful before any identity request. A visitor's choice
to identify or associate an approved result/reference requires a later
versioned consent, retention, withdrawal, and deletion decision.

Do not ask about budget or procurement in the reflection. That conversation
belongs only in a later, voluntary identified follow-up path, if separately
approved. This direction does not authorize automated email, any other email
workflow, or outreach.

## Prohibited information and inferences

The first reflection must not request or accept:

- names, emails, organizations, titles, or LinkedIn identifiers;
- patient/client information or details about an individual employee;
- diagnoses, treatment, crisis, safety-incident, disciplinary, performance,
  leave, accommodation, or employment records;
- personal mindfulness history, mental-health information, or health
  inference;
- staffing levels, turnover figures, identifiable workforce complaints, or
  sensitive free text; or
- uploaded files, URLs, raw invitation tokens, or analytics identifiers.

Do not infer that the visitor is the original recipient, that a completion is
buyer intent, or that any answer describes the organization as a whole.
PostHog may later receive only the approved anonymous milestone—not answers,
result content, free text, PII, raw tokens, organizations, or inferred health
information.
