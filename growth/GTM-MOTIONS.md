# Growth — GTM Motions

Last updated: 2026-07-15 16:59:36 PDT — edited by: Codex

GTM motions are reusable approaches for learning or reaching a market. They
are separate from segments and hypotheses: the same motion can test different
hypotheses, and the same hypothesis may later require more than one motion.
This file records strategy context only; it never authorizes contact or
outbound activity. [DR-29](../decisions/2026-07-13-segment-linked-runs.md)

## GTM-001 — Account-led buyer discovery

- Purpose: Use cited public account intelligence to identify a small set of
  organizations and the buyer-group roles relevant to a hypothesis, then
  prepare one manual discovery test only after Mark approves its Run 003
  packet.
- Suitable for: B2B organization-sponsored hypotheses with a named segment,
  plausible buyer role, and a bounded learning question.
- Inputs: public organization pages, strategy/workforce materials, job
  postings, public leadership or category content, and published competitor
  positioning.
- Output: account cards, buyer-role map, language/category map, a proposed
  test, and contradictions—not a contact list or an inference of intent.
- Boundaries: no scraping, continuous monitoring, contact enrichment, or
  outreach. Public data cannot establish budget or willingness to pay.

### Proposed extension — decision-maker-team and public activity qualification

- Intent: Starting with the registry's highest research-priority accounts,
  map the *roles* in a plausible buying group: a primary decision-maker role
  plus likely clinical, workforce/HR, operations, quality, finance, IT/security,
  procurement, or implementation counterparts. This is a buying-process
  hypothesis, not a claim that any person has authority.
- Decision supported: Decide whether two to three refreshed accounts have a
  sufficiently visible, role-based buying-team hypothesis and publicly
  observable conversation surface to justify a separately proposed manual
  discovery packet.
- Evidence approach: A future bounded public-research run may verify current
  organization and team-role context from official sources, then assess only
  point-in-time, publicly observable LinkedIn or other social activity as a
  *conversation-channel signal*. Record the observation date, public source,
  role/function, and explicit unknowns; do not treat activity as interest,
  authority, budget, or willingness to buy.
- Provider gate: Any LinkedIn API, authenticated access, paid data provider,
  or platform-specific data collection requires a named-provider review and
  distinct Mark approval before use. The default is manual review of permitted
  public sources; no LinkedIn API is authorized by this motion.
- Prohibited: Personal contact details, private/profile-dossier collection,
  automated collection or scraping, continuous monitoring, scoring people,
  CRM writes, direct messages, connection requests, outreach, or publishing.
  Do not retain names unless a later Mark-approved run explicitly changes the
  registry's personal-data boundary.
